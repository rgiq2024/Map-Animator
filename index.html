How to draw
  1. Click to place points. The line curves smoothly through them by itself.
  2. Click on the line to add a point in between.
  3. Double-click a point to make it a sharp corner with straight lines. Double-click again to curve it.
  4. Drag a point to move it.
  5. Click a point for more: pause here, split the leg, or delete it.
  6. Legs (top right) set how you travel: walk, car, ferry, plane… Add a new leg to switch.

                  How to make a map video

                  📘 Open the illustrated tutorials — every element, step by step

                  1. Route. Click on the map to place points, or load a GPX / KML / KMZ / IGC file. The line curves smoothly through your points. Click the line to add a point, double-click a point for a sharp corner, drag points to move them, and click a point to pause there or split the leg.
                  2. Legs. Each leg has a way of travelling — walk, car, train, ferry, plane and more — with its own symbol and line style. Use + New leg to switch.
                  3. Layers. Draw or import boundaries, areas, floods, lines, points, animated symbols, text and captions. Each has its own animation and timing — trace a boundary, spread a flood from a point, raise water over real terrain, pulse a symbol, type out a caption.
                  4. Space & defence. In the Layers step add satellite passes, the ISS orbit, launch sites, 3D ships/jets/drones, missile and drone paths, blasts, bombing runs and capture areas — plus charts, counters, HUD overlays and flicker text. Add 3D model (built-in or Upload GLB / glTF) or an Effect clip video. Select an item and drag its square handle to resize it, the round handle to rotate it.
                  5. Auto video. Press ✨ Auto video (home page or top bar), describe the video in a sentence — places, date and what happened, or the exact latitude, longitude — press Find the places, check the plan, then Create the video. You get an opening, a news-explainer map, the event in 3D and an end card, all editable.
                  6. Story & text callouts. Layers → Story & text callouts: callouts tied to a place, photo carousel / fan / helix / orbit / shatter / polaroid stack (add your photos in the layer panel), and match-cut article highlights. Drag the BEFORE / AFTER tags of a before/after layer wherever you want them.
                  7. Auto video building blocks. In ✨ Auto video, press Find the places, then tick what you want: route A → B (road, railway, flight, ship), highlight with ripples, affected area, info callout, before / after images, HUD, weather, ticker, music.
                  8. Drag legends and text. Select a layer, then drag its legend, panel or text box anywhere on the video.
                  9. Logo & lower third. Shown on every scene by default. Change the country, place, date and ticker text (separate items with |), colour and font, replace the logo strip, or switch either off in Preview & export → Logo & lower third.
                  10. Cinematic HUD pack. Layers → Cinematic HUD pack: satellite launch countdown, HUD ring title, HUD name card, biometric scanner, sci-fi data console, live-feed scanner, holo table grid and scope view.
                  11. Sound. Sound is off until you turn it on in Preview & export → Sound; then pick a score such as Epic cinematic intro trailer or Epic cinematic (heroic).
                  12. News-style graphics. Showcase elements → tag labels (type “|” in the title to stack boxes), violet / night tints, highlight tiles, mosaic tint, storm vortex, ship track, photo card, AR panel, line callouts, vehicle detection, plus-mark grid and sun lens flare.
                  13. Analytics & knowledge graph. Showcase elements → Real-time analytics dashboard (edit the numbers, callout, chips in the layer panel; add a point layer for the callout position — it looks best over a Hologram digital-twin city) and Knowledge graph (one node per line: “Name | type | linked to”).
                  14. Showcase elements. Layers → Showcase elements: spectral split and false-colour/thermal maps, satellite scan HUD, hologram projection, code screen, split screen with your pictures, a map diorama block (tilt the camera to see its sides) with floating layer plates, wildlife and weather markers, icon badges, flow lines, light beams, network mesh, viewshed (zoom in on a city with buildings) and a drone video footprint with its player window.
                  15. Military & intel HUD. Layers → Military & intel HUD: a GEOINT drone feed, a command & control video wall, an operations dashboard, a signals intercept and Morse code text. Edit the numbers, pie chart, event log and screen names in the layer panel; Morse is keyed and played at the speed you choose. Military sounds (radar, lock-on, missile warning, radio chatter, siren…) play automatically and are in the sound library.
                  16. Smart maps. Layers → Smart maps: press Night roads map, then add a data layer (population mosaic, income hot spots, potential sites, drive-time areas, projected revenue…) and draw the region it fills. Load your own CSV or GeoJSON points in the layer's Data section, add an Animated legend and a 3D info card. Or try Auto video with “Best location for a new store in Texas”.
                  17. Digital twin. Layers → Digital twin: zoom in to a city, pick a look (white model, hologram, blueprint…) and click the centre — the real buildings rise from there with light trails on the roads. Add Live data cards on top, a Plane taking off (click along the runway, then into the sky) or a Ship arriving at port. Tilt the camera in the Camera step.
                  18. People & hazards. Layers → People & crowds for crowds, protest marches, people fleeing or gathering; Hazard zones for nuclear / explosion blast rings, a spreading wildfire, deforestation over the years, and Affected buildings — only the real buildings inside the zone are raised in 3D (zoom in to a city and tilt the camera).
                  19. Green screen. Effects & video → Green-screen video removes the green background of your clip (e.g. a presenter). Or pick the Green screen base map in Look to key the whole animation out in your editor.
                  20. Train crash. In Layers → 3D models: Train collision (head-on), Train derails into another or Train rear-end crash. Zoom in close (the trains are real size), draw the line of the track, press Finish, and tilt the camera. Pick the colours, number of cars, run-up and how violent it is.
                  21. Network analysis. In Layers → Network analysis: click a place for a drive / walk / cycle-time area; click A, then B for a route that races car, bike, walking, train and plane; click an origin and then destinations for OD flows; outline a zone for a geofence with a vehicle that triggers alerts. While drawing, use the bar at the bottom of the map: Undo point, Finish or Cancel. A route finishes by itself after you click B. Type a place name in the layer settings to move a point there.
                  22. Quick add. Press / anywhere, type what you want (“isochrone”, “cyclone”, “3D train”) and press Enter. The Layers step also has a search box and folding categories.
                  23. 3D models. In Layers → 3D models: a train, car or ship collision, a 3D city that grows up in an area, trains on a track, bombers, ships, the Global Hawk drone, NASA satellites, skyscrapers, wind turbines and oil rigs. Bombing runs and missile paths can use a 3D aircraft too — pick it under 3D model in their settings.
                  24. Imagery. Add a georeferenced image (PNG/JPG with its world file, a GeoTIFF or a KML/KMZ overlay) or any picture and line it up by dragging its corners. Use Before / after to swipe between two images, Images through time for a year-by-year sequence, or Satellite through time for archived Esri imagery.
                  25. Weather. In Layers → Weather: draw a cyclone track point by point (set each point's category), place L / H pressure centres and fronts, drop city weather markers, add temperature, rainfall or wind over the view (made-up pattern or live data), and forecast panels or a warning banner. Live satellite shows the latest GOES / Meteosat / Himawari picture (or a loop of the last hours) to follow a cyclone; Live radar shows rain; Live weather now puts current weather and a forecast on the cities in view.
                  26. Film & TV. Add opening/cinematic/end titles, rolling credits, a channel logo, BREAKING NEWS and a ticker from Titles & TV news; cinema bars are in Look. Add your own video, film grain, VHS, glitch, rain or camera shake from Effects & video. Drop sounds from the sound library in Preview & export.
                  27. Speed & tracks. Click a leg to change its speed or pick a 3D track (railway, bus lane, road, flight arc). Use the Slower / Faster buttons on any layer.
                  28. Timeline edits. Shift + drag across the timeline and press Delete to cut a section out; Freeze holds the picture at the playhead; +1 s makes the scene longer. Captions, charts and HUD text can be dragged anywhere on the video.
                  29. Sound. Cinematic sound effects are added automatically — explosions, aircraft, HUD beeps, wind when the camera zooms. Pick a background score and volumes in Preview & export; switch sound off for any layer in its settings.
                  30. Globe. Turn on the globe in Look, then pick “Earth zoom from space” in Camera. Search a place or type coordinates like 25.2, 55.27 to fly there.
                  31. Look. Pick a map theme, place names, 3D mountains, line colour and symbol.
                  32. Signs. Add boards, flags, photos and more. They pop up when the line reaches them.
                  33. Camera. Film a fixed view, glide from a start view to an end view, or follow the line.
                  34. Preview & export. Set the stills and resolution, then render an MP4 right in your browser.

                  Use scenes for separate parts of a trip; they are joined into one video. Drag scene tabs to reorder them, ✕ to remove one, and set a scene's length (up to many minutes) under the timeline. Keyboard: Space play/pause, Ctrl+Z undo, Delete remove the selected point or sign.

                  FAQ

                  Is it free? Do I need an account?

                  Yes, it's free, and there's no account. Your projects are saved in this browser only.

                  Where does my video get made?

                  In your browser, frame by frame. Nothing is uploaded. Keep the tab open while it renders.

                  Which browsers work?

                  Recent Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox. Chrome and Edge make H.264 MP4s that play everywhere.

                  How do I move a project to another computer?

                  Use Save file in the editor, then open the file on the other computer.

                  Can I use the videos commercially?

                  Yes. Map credits are off in the video by default; the map and imagery providers ask to be credited, so turn Map credits in video on in Preview & export, or credit them in your video description.

                  About

                  GIS One Map Animator is a tool for making animated maps. It runs entirely in your browser using MapLibre GL, OpenStreetMap data via OpenFreeMap, Esri imagery, AWS terrain tiles, Nominatim search and FOSSGIS OSRM routing.
